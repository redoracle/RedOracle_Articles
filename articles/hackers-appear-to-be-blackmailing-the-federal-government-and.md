# Hackers Blackmail Swiss Federal Government After Data Breach

## At a glance

Hackers are blackmailing the Swiss federal government following a devastating data breach at the software supplier PK Softech, which provides services to the federal pension fund Publica. The hacker group Akira is demanding a ransom after stealing sensitive information, with potential exposure of data about undercover police officers and intelligence agents. The Swiss government has classified the incident as serious, particularly for employees of the intelligence services, the military, and Fedpol, due to the risk that compromised data could endanger these individuals abroad [[1](#ref-1)][[3](#ref-3)][[6](#ref-6)].

## What happened

A cyberattack on PK Softech, a software company near Basel that serves the federal pension fund Publica, triggered a massive data leak. The breach affected more than 100,000 insured individuals, including personal data such as AHV numbers, salaries, and account numbers of pension fund members [[1](#ref-1)][[6](#ref-6)][[7](#ref-7)]. The stolen data may also include information about undercover police officers and intelligence agents, raising significant concerns for national security [[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)][[6](#ref-6)].

## Confirmed impact

The impact on individuals is substantial. More than 100,000 insured persons (primarily Pension Fund members and, potentially, staff at affiliated organizations such as ETH) had their personal data compromised. This includes uniquely identifiable information like AHV numbers (Swiss pensions insurance numbers), salaries, and bank account numbers [[1](#ref-1)][[6](#ref-6)][[7](#ref-7)]. The breach extended beyond Publica to other institutions indirectly connected through shared infrastructure, with reports indicating that nearly a dozen other pension funds and federal agencies were affected [[4](#ref-4)].

## How the attack worked

The exact technical mechanism by which the attack was executed has not been publicly disclosed. Sources provide no concrete details about the infiltration method, the entry point into PK Softech's systems, or how the hackers obtained access to sensitive data [[5](#ref-5)][[7](#ref-7)]. While reports indicate that the attacker compromised a third-party software provider rather than the pension fund directly, the precise chain of events remains undetermined. This lack of technical detail limits the ability of defenders to assess specific vulnerabilities or attribute responsibility clearly [[2](#ref-2)][[6](#ref-6)].

## What defenders should do

The available reports do not offer concrete, actionable recommendations for defenders or government agencies. While the situation is described as serious and the government has launched an investigation, there is no detailed guidance on immediate containment, forensic response, or mitigation steps for similar incidents [[4](#ref-4)][[5](#ref-5)]. Public statements from Publica, the software supplier, and federal authorities focus primarily on notification, investigation, and reassurance rather than on specific defensive measures [[3](#ref-3)][[6](#ref-6)]. Consequently, the article does not provide a set of concrete defensive actions that can be applied by organizations facing similar threats [[7](#ref-7)].

## What remains unknown

Several critical aspects of the incident remain unclear. It is not yet certain which specific data elements were compromised beyond the confirmed 100,000+ insured individuals, nor is it known whether the ransom demanded by the Akira group has been paid or is still outstanding [[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)]. The scope of the leak to other pension funds and government agencies is also not fully quantified [[4](#ref-4)][[5](#ref-5)]. Additionally, the identity of the software supplier PK Softech has not been disclosed by the affected parties, and the full extent of the breach to other external organizations remains uncertain [[5](#ref-5)][[7](#ref-7)]. These uncertainties complicate both internal response efforts and external investigative work [[1](#ref-1)][[3](#ref-3)].
