# Romanian Intelligence Agency Says It Identified Russian-Linked Cyber Attack

## At a glance

The Romanian Intelligence Service (SRI) announced on Wednesday that it had identified and halted a cyberattack against a Romanian government institution. The agency's National Cyberint Center detected the intrusion in December 2025 and took measures to contain the threat and address its effects [[1](#ref-1)]. According to the report, attackers exploited multiple vulnerabilities in Microsoft operating systems in an attempt to compromise the institution's IT infrastructure [[2](#ref-2)]. The attack affected the institution's email service, which was subsequently restored after the intervention of specialists [[1](#ref-1)]. SRI confirmed that no sensitive data was extracted from the compromised systems [[1](#ref-1)]. Importantly, the targeted government institution was not formally identified by SRI [[3](#ref-3)]. The cyberattack was attributed to the Russian group APT Turla, which is widely linked to Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) [[3](#ref-3)]. Romanian authorities have shared information about the incident with relevant national bodies and intelligence partners in Europe, including through cooperation at the EU and NATO levels [[1](#ref-1)]. The National Cyberint Center is also investigating other incidents connected to cyber campaigns carried out by Russia [[2](#ref-2)].

## What happened

The National Cyberint Center of the SRI detected the intrusion in December 2025 and quickly intervened to neutralize the threat [[1](#ref-1)]. The attackers, identified as the APT Turla group, attempted to compromise the IT systems of a Romanian government institution. APT Turla, also known by several aliases including Snake, Uroburos, Venomous Bear and Secret Blizzard, has been active since at least 2004 and has been associated with operations targeting government agencies, diplomatic institutions and other organizations [[3](#ref-3)]. The attackers exploited several systemic vulnerabilities within Microsoft operating systems as part of their campaign to compromise the institution's IT infrastructure [[2](#ref-2)]. The affected email service was subsequently restored following the intervention of SRI specialists [[1](#ref-1)]. According to SRI, investigators found no evidence that sensitive data had been extracted from the compromised systems [[1](#ref-1)]. The agency stated that the targeted government institution was not identified in their investigation [[3](#ref-3)]. Romania's domestic intelligence service said on November 11 that it had stopped a cyberattack by the Russian hacking group APT Turla against a Romanian government agency [[1](#ref-1)]. The SRI also disclosed that the National Cyberint Center is conducting ongoing investigations into other incidents linked to cyber campaigns carried out by Russia [[2](#ref-2)].

## Why it matters

This incident is significant because it represents another example of Russian-origin cyber operations targeting Romanian state institutions [[1](#ref-1)]. The successful thwarting of the attack by the SRI demonstrates the agency's capability to detect and respond to sophisticated threats [[1](#ref-1)]. The fact that the attackers exploited vulnerabilities in Microsoft operating systems highlights the importance of maintaining robust patch management and system hardening across government networks [[2](#ref-2)]. The restoration of the email service shows that the attackers were able to cause operational disruption without achieving their ultimate objective of data exfiltration [[1](#ref-1)]. The absence of sensitive data extraction is a positive outcome, though it underscores the need for continuous monitoring following such incidents [[1](#ref-1)]. The identification of APT Turla as the responsible group provides concrete attribution, which aids in understanding the broader threat landscape and informs defensive postures across the country [[3](#ref-3)]. The collaboration with European partners through the EU and NATO levels indicates that this is part of a larger regional security framework [[1](#ref-1)]. The ongoing investigations by the SRI into other Russian-linked incidents suggest that the threat is persistent and that the country's intelligence community remains vigilant [[2](#ref-2)].

## Technical details

The attackers employed multiple techniques exploiting weaknesses in Microsoft operating systems to gain a foothold in the target institution's network [[2](#ref-2)]. This multi-vector approach is characteristic of advanced persistent threat (APT) groups that aim to maintain long-term access to critical infrastructure [[3](#ref-3)]. The National Cyberint Center's detection in December 2025 indicates that the attackers likely gained initial access before the agency's intervention [[1](#ref-1)]. While the exact initial access method is not specified in the available reports, the focus of the attack appears to have been on the email service and related IT systems [[1](#ref-1)]. The fact that the email service was restored after the intervention suggests that the attackers may have relied on lateral movement within the network rather than direct exploitation of the email service itself [[1](#ref-1)]. SRI's investigation confirmed that no sensitive data was extracted from the compromised systems, which is a positive outcome but does not diminish the importance of the attack as a potential precursor to future operations [[1](#ref-1)]. The involvement of APT Turla, a group with a long history of cyber espionage, suggests that the attack was part of a broader campaign targeting multiple government entities [[3](#ref-3)]. The SRI continues to investigate other incidents connected to cyber campaigns carried out by Russia, indicating that this thwarted attack is part of a larger pattern of state-sponsored activity [[2](#ref-2)].

## What defenders should do

Defenders of Romanian government institutions should maintain rigorous patch management across Microsoft operating systems to prevent similar exploitation of known vulnerabilities [[2](#ref-2)]. The successful response by the SRI demonstrates the value of timely threat detection and the importance of having a coordinated incident response plan [[1](#ref-1)]. Organizations should conduct regular security assessments to identify and remediate potential weaknesses in their IT infrastructure [[1](#ref-1)]. Since the targeted institution was not formally identified by SRI, defensive measures should also consider that the attack could be directed at other government entities with similar configurations [[3](#ref-3)]. The restoration of the email service without data loss highlights that network segmentation and access controls can be effective countermeasures against email-focused campaigns [[1](#ref-1)]. Given that APT Turla is linked to Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) and has previously targeted diplomatic and government organizations [[3](#ref-3)], continuity of operations planning should account for possible disruption to critical communications [[2](#ref-2)]. The SRI's cooperation with EU and NATO intelligence partners underscores the importance of sharing threat intelligence across borders to improve collective defense capabilities [[1](#ref-1)]. Defenders should also remain alert to the possibility of follow-on attacks or additional phases of the campaign, as the SRI is still investigating other incidents connected to Russian cyber campaigns [[2](#ref-2)]. Ultimately, a combination of technical controls, threat intelligence sharing, and rapid incident response remains the best approach to mitigating the impact of such sophisticated attacks [[1](#ref-1)].
