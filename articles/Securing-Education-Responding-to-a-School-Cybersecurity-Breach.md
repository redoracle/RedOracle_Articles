# Securing Education Responding to a School Cybersecurity Breach

![Image](https://storage.googleapis.com/red_articles/Securing-Education-Responding-to-a-School-Cybersecurity-Breach.png)

## Overview

Laurens County School District 56 has transitioned to traditional learning methods following a significant cybersecurity breach that impacted its computer systems. The district is now utilizing pencils, papers, and textbooks instead of electronic devices to ensure the safety and security of student data and educational activities.

## Key Details

- **When:** The decision to switch to non-digital methods was made effective on February 3, 2025, after the breach was confirmed.
- **Where:** The incident is taking place within the Laurens County School District 56 in South Carolina.
- **Why:** The primary goal of the switch is to safeguard student data and maintain educational continuity amidst ongoing assessments of the breach.
- **Who:** District Superintendent, administrators, students, and state and local law enforcement are involved in responding to the cybersecurity incident.

## Response Plan

- **Immediate Actions:** 
  - Notification of the breach to law enforcement.
  - Implementation of alternative teaching methods using traditional resources like paper and textbooks.
  - Continued assessment of impacted computer systems to determine necessary restorations.

## Previous Incidents

- **PowerSchool Breach:** 
  - Last month, the district was part of a national cybersecurity incident involving PowerSchool, resulting in significant risks to student data.
  - More details available in a separate report on the compromise.

## Conclusion

As schools adapt to the challenges posed by cyber threats, Laurens County School District 56 remains committed to ensuring educational continuity and data protection through traditional methods. Stay updated with ongoing developments regarding this cybersecurity incident as the district takes necessary steps to rectify and understand the situation further.
