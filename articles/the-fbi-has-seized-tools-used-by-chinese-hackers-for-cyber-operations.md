# FBI Seizes Tools Used by Chinese Hackers for Cyber Operations: A Blow to Indiscriminate and Reckless Hacking

## At a glance

According to reports [1-5], the FBI has seized tools, known as 'Microscan' and 'FishHub', used by Chinese hackers for cyber operations. These tools were employed to target various entities, including a U.S. power company, Japanese and Polish airports, Taiwanese universities, a multinational, nongovernmental organization, and Taiwanese critical infrastructure companies [[1](#ref-1)]. The latest operation by the FBI has rendered the tools inoperable, making it harder for China-linked hackers to target American networks [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)].

## What happened

The FBI's seizure of the Chinese hacking tools came as part of an ongoing effort to disrupt the broad-based hacking campaign known as Flax Typhoon [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)]. The tools, Microscan and FishHub, were used by the hackers to scan, phish, and hack targets [[1](#ref-1)]. Microscan was used to target entities such as power companies in the U.S., Japanese and Polish airports, Taiwanese universities, a multinational, nongovernmental organization, and Taiwanese critical infrastructure companies [[1](#ref-1)]. FishHub facilitated phishing activity, providing unauthorized access to victim networks [[1](#ref-1)].

The FBI identified Integrity Technology Group, a Chinese-based information security company, as the operator of the tools [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)]. The FBI considers Integrity Technology Group to be the true identity of Flax Typhoon [[1](#ref-1)]. In September 2024, the FBI announced the disruption of a massive botnet associated with Flax Typhoon, which installed malicious software on over 200,000 consumer devices [[2](#ref-2)].

## Why it matters

The seizure of the Chinese hacking tools represents a significant blow to the hacking operation, as the tools have been rendered inoperable [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)]. This disruption of the hacking infrastructure makes it harder for China-linked hackers to target American networks [[1](#ref-1)]. Hackers often rely on contractors and other companies to expand the reach of their cyber operations [[5](#ref-5)]. Disrupting these organizations can significantly impact the ability of hackers to carry out attacks on critical infrastructure.
