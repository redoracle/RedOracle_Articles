## At a glance

Chinese hackers used DeepSeek and other AI tools in sweeping global cyberattacks, report finds [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)][[4](#ref-4)][[5](#ref-5)]

The use of artificial intelligence (AI) by Chinese hackers in global cyberattacks targeting over 200 organizations [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)][[4](#ref-4)] is raising concerns. This section provides a brief overview of the incidents reported by various sources.

## What happened

In October 2026, several sources reported that Chinese hackers used artificial intelligence, specifically the DeepSeek model, to target over 200 organizations worldwide [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)]. The attacks, which occurred in just 11 days, illustrate how AI is enabling hackers to operate at a scale previously requiring more human effort [[1](#ref-1)].

The targeted organizations spanned various sectors, including technology and finance, and were located on multiple continents [[2](#ref-2)]. The hackers executed more than 200 intrusion attempts, a volume that would have required weeks of manual work [[2](#ref-2)]. The use of DeepSeek marked a shift in how state-linked groups integrate commercial AI into their offensive operations [[2](#ref-2)].

Further reports indicated that a Chinese-speaking hacker used AI tools to breach several South Korean financial institutions and steal data between late September and early October [[5](#ref-5)]. The attacks targeted a bank's loan inquiry service and another lender's employee mobile-support platform [[5](#ref-5)].

## Why it matters

The scale and the reported findings from the sources indicate the significance of these incidents [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)][[4](#ref-4)]. The use of AI in cyberattacks allows for increased efficiency and speed, enabling hackers to target more organizations and potentially cause more damage in a shorter period [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)].

## Technical details

The DeepSeek AI model was used to accelerate reconnaissance and craft malicious payloads [[2](#ref-2)]. The hackers leveraged the mobile app, which has drawn global attention since its release, to generate phishing content, analyze target networks, and refine social engineering tactics [[2](#ref-2)]. By adopting an existing model, the attackers lowered the barrier to entry for large-scale campaigns [[2](#ref-2)].
