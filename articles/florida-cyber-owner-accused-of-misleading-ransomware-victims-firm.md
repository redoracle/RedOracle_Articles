# Florida Cybersecurity Firm Owner Accused of Misleading Ransomware Victims, Secretly Paying Hackers $8M

## At a glance

Zohar Pinhasi, the owner of Florida-based cybersecurity firm MonsterCloud, has been accused of misleading ransomware victims and secretly paying hackers while billing clients for premium decryption services. The alleged five-year scheme netted over $19 million from hundreds of companies, including over $8 million in ransom payments [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)]. The indictment against Pinhasi was unsealed on September 23, and he appeared in federal court for arraignment on October 8 [[4](#ref-4)].

## What happened

Pinhasi, 50, who also went by Zack Silver and Zack Green, allegedly convinced victims that MonsterCloud could decrypt their files using proprietary tools, while in reality, the company was secretly paying off ransomware attackers [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)][[3](#ref-3)]. According to the indictment, MonsterCloud presented decrypted sample files obtained from attackers as proof of its own capabilities [[2](#ref-2)]. Pinhasi reportedly admitted in 2019 that the company held no proprietary decryption technology and privately described the recovery process as a trade secret involving "proprietary means and methods" [[3](#ref-3)].

## Why it matters

The accusations against Pinhasi and MonsterCloud highlight the growing trust issues in cybersecurity recovery services [[1](#ref-1)]. Companies are increasingly reliant on third-party services to recover from ransomware attacks, and the incidents described in this article demonstrate the importance of transparency and honesty in these services.

## Technical details

One example of MonsterCloud's alleged activities is an August 2023 case where Pinhasi paid a hacker roughly $8,200 but billed the client about $150,000 [[2](#ref-2)]. Another instance occurred around October 2021, where he paid about $236,000 in ransom and charged the customer roughly $380,000 [[3](#ref-3)].
