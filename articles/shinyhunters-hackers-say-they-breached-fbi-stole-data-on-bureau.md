# ShinyHunters hackers say they breached the FBI, stole data on employees

## At a glance

The digital extortion group ShinyHunters has claimed to have breached the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) and stolen sensitive data on thousands of bureau employees [[1](#ref-1)]. The FBI is investigating these claims [[1](#ref-1)], while media reports suggest that the group accessed about 2 to 3 terabytes of data linked to the FBI and its systems [[4](#ref-4)]. The breach reportedly included personal details such as names, home addresses, phone numbers, and information about spouses [[2](#ref-2)], [[3](#ref-3)]. This incident highlights significant vulnerabilities in the security of federal employee records [[1](#ref-1)].

## What happened

ShinyHunters, a prolific cybercriminal collective, announced on Tuesday that it had compromised FBI-related systems and exfiltrated a large amount of personnel data [[1](#ref-1)], [[3](#ref-3)]. The group stated in a message to its dark-web platform and during an online chat with Reuters that it targeted the FBI in retaliation for a May 2026 agency announcement detailing the group's methods and urging targets not to pay [[2](#ref-2)], [[3](#ref-3)], [[5](#ref-5)]. According to the claim, ShinyHunters obtained data on "almost all FBI agents and individuals" [[1](#ref-1)], and their documentation described the stolen information as including FBI agents' names, home addresses, phone numbers, and details about their spouses [[2](#ref-2)], [[3](#ref-3)].

The FBI has acknowledged that unauthorized activity affecting its systems is under investigation [[1](#ref-1)], [[5](#ref-5)]. The agency's spokesperson confirmed awareness of the claims and is actively looking into the matter [[5](#ref-5)]. The breach originated from compromised third-party software, with ShinyHunters first accessing an Oracle PeopleSoft server used by human resources and recruitment functions before moving to an Amazon-hosted government cloud storing personal data on FBI agents and applicants [[6](#ref-6)], [[4](#ref-4)]. They also defaced the FBI's Special Agent Applicant Portal, which was reported as being "currently down for maintenance" at the time [[3](#ref-3)], [[5](#ref-5)]. ShinyHunters publicly shared a spreadsheet containing the personal information of roughly 5,000 alleged FBI officials, including job assignments, addresses, phone numbers, and emergency contacts, and verified that some details matched public records [[2](#ref-2)], [[3](#ref-3)].

## Why it matters

This breach extends beyond the individuals affected. The FBI is the nation's primary investigative law enforcement agency, and unauthorized access to its personnel database poses a high-profile counterintelligence risk [[1](#ref-1)]. The group's demand for the FBI to "correct or simply REMOVE" the advisory it published earlier adds pressure on the agency to respond quickly [[3](#ref-3)]. If the stolen data falls into the wrong hands, it could be used to coerce or extort FBI agents and their families, potentially jeopardizing ongoing investigations and national security operations [[6](#ref-6)].

The scale of the data exfiltration (estimated at 2 to 3 terabytes) indicates a sophisticated operation capable of compromising substantial portions of federal employee records [[4](#ref-4)]. This is the second known breach of an FBI system this year, following an earlier incident involving an FBI job portal [[1](#ref-1)]. The combination of the target's institutional importance, the breadth of the stolen data, and the potential for misuse makes this incident particularly significant in the context of rising cybersecurity threats [[5](#ref-5)]. Experts warn that such breaches could enable adversaries to identify and target individuals for intimidation or blackmail, undermining public trust in federal institutions [[6](#ref-6)].

## Technical details

ShinyHunters executed a multi-stage attack that began with compromising an Oracle PeopleSoft server, a system widely used by human resources and recruitment departments to manage job applicant information [[6](#ref-6)]. The attackers then moved to an Amazon-hosted government cloud environment storing sensitive data on FBI agents and job applicants [[6](#ref-6)]. This two-pronged approach allowed them to access both applicant records and agent records [[6](#ref-6)].

The stolen data included full names, home addresses, phone numbers, and information about spouses and other close contacts [[2](#ref-2)], [[3](#ref-3)]. The group's methodology involved leveraging compromised third-party software to gain initial access, followed by targeted exploitation of internal systems [[6](#ref-6)]. While the exact technical implementation remains undisclosed, the pattern of access and the volume of data extracted suggest a well-coordinated operation with insider or advanced persistent threat capabilities [[6](#ref-6)]. The defacement of the FBI's jobs site further indicates the group's intent to disrupt access to critical information resources [[3](#ref-3)].
