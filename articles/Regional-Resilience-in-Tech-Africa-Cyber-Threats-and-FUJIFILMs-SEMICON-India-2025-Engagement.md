## Introduction

This article synthesizes two parallel developments in August 2025 that highlight regional resilience across digital infrastructure and advanced manufacturing. The first is a Kaspersky analysis of evolving cyberthreats in Africa as the continent expands connectivity ahead of Gitex Nigeria 2025. The second is FUJIFILM Corporation's announced participation in SEMICON India 2025 as part of a strategic effort to localize semiconductor materials manufacturing and build integrated supply chain capability in India. Both items illustrate the intersection of threat intelligence, risk management, investment, partnerships, policy, and ecosystem development across africa, nigeria, gitex, semicon, india, fujifilm, materials, manufacturing, ecosystem, supplychain, threatintelligence, riskmanagement, digitaleconomy, partnerships, policy, investment, resilience, regionalgrowth, events.

## Executive Overview

- Two developments capture regional resilience themes.
  - Kaspersky presents a continentwide cyberthreat assessment focused on risk priorities ahead of Gitex Nigeria 2025.
  - FUJIFILM announces plans to showcase and advance its semiconductor materials portfolio at SEMICON India 2025 in New Delhi, with explicit intentions to establish a local manufacturing base and integrated supply chain in India.
- Both developments emphasize proactive preparedness: defensive posture and operational capacity for Africa, and materials localization and partnership building for India.
- Key strategic drivers include rising digital adoption, event driven exposure, government policy support, and projected market growth for semiconductor manufacturing in India.

## 1. Kaspersky Assessment: Africa Cyberthreat Landscape Ahead of Gitex Nigeria 2025

### Who

- Kaspersky Lab threat intelligence teams.
- Intended audiences include corporate security teams, government agencies, event organizers, technology partners, and operators across Africa and Nigeria.

### What

- A focused assessment of evolving cyberthreats across Africa with emphasis on trends likely to impact Gitex Nigeria 2025 and other high visibility technology gatherings.
- Emphasis on emerging attacker techniques, expanded attack surface, and event related threat scenarios that can target enterprises, public sector entities, and event infrastructure.

### When

- Analysis anchored in 2025 timeframe, prepared ahead of Gitex Nigeria 2025 to inform near term mitigation and readiness.

### Where

- Continental Africa with a focal point on Nigeria and the ecosystem surrounding Gitex Nigeria 2025.

### Why

- The purpose is to enable stakeholders to anticipate and prioritize controls, incident response readiness, workforce training, and cross border information sharing to protect digital services and event ecosystems during high exposure periods.

### How

- Kaspersky uses telemetry, threat intelligence workflows, and regional incident pattern synthesis to identify priorities. The underlying approach is practical and oriented to defensive preparedness for events and expanding digital services.

### Key threat patterns and priorities

- Expanded attack surface as connectivity and digital services grow.
- Increased phishing and credential theft campaigns tied to event communications and registration flows.
- Ransomware and extortion threats against critical providers and venues.
- Supply chain risk targeting third party vendors supporting events and services.
- Mobile malware and targeted campaigns against high profile attendees or institutions.
- Potential for nation state or advanced persistent threat actors to exploit geopolitical or infrastructure weaknesses.

### Recommended risk focus areas

- Continuous monitoring and telemetry integration for event infrastructure.
- Hardened identity and access management for event platforms.
- Pre event tabletop exercises and incident response playbooks.
- Cross industry and cross border information sharing to accelerate detection and mitigation.
- Targeted workforce awareness campaigns for vendors, attendees, and event staff.

## 2. FUJIFILM at SEMICON India 2025

### Who

- FUJIFILM Corporation and FUJIFILM India.
- Koji Wada, Managing Director, FUJIFILM India, quoted on corporate purpose and regional strategy.
- Indian semiconductor customers, industry partners, government initiatives, and trade associations such as JETRO referenced as ecosystem context.

### What

- FUJIFILM participates in SEMICON India 2025, an international exhibition focused on semiconductor manufacturing equipment and materials.
- The company presents a broad semiconductor materials portfolio spanning wafer processing through post processing, positioning itself as a one stop supplier for materials and process needs.

### When

- SEMICON India 2025 scheduled for September 2 to 4, 2025.
- Announcement and coverage appear in late August 2025.

### Where

- New Delhi, India.

### Why

- India’s semiconductor market is projected to exceed 100 billion dollars by 2030, driven by government policy incentives and strategic investment in domestic capability.
- FUJIFILM aims to deepen engagement with customers, forge partnerships, and accelerate its semiconductor materials business in India by establishing local manufacturing and an integrated supply chain.

### How

- FUJIFILM showcases and offers materials including:
  - Photoresists and photolithography materials for patterning.
  - CMP slurries and post CMP cleaners for planarization and residue removal.
  - Thin film chemicals, dielectric films, and polyimides for protective and redistribution layers.
  - High purity process chemicals for cleaning and etching.
  - WAVE CONTROL MOSAIC, a family of materials for color filters in image sensors.
- Strategy centers on localizing production from raw material procurement to finished product supply to improve resilience and reduce dependency on external suppliers.

### Leadership perspective

- Koji Wada stated that FUJIFILM India is committed to delivering innovative products and solutions aligned with the group purpose of Giving Our World More Smiles and that participation in SEMICON India 2025 reaffirms the company’s commitment to technology and to Building the Next Semiconductor Powerhouse through collaboration and local partnership.

## Materials and Intellectual Property Notes

- Photoresists for circuit patterning.
- Photolithography related chemicals to enable fine patterning and process control.
- CMP slurries to achieve planarization and surface uniformity.
- Post CMP cleaners to remove residues and particles.
- Thin film chemicals and dielectric films for interlayer insulation and device performance.
- Polyimides used for protective layers and redistribution layer formation.
- High purity process chemicals for etch and clean processes.
- WAVE CONTROL MOSAIC trademark refers to color filter materials for CMOS image sensors.

## Detailed Analysis

- India market projection to exceed 100 billion dollars by 2030 provides a commercial rationale for materials localization and integrated supplychain investments. Establishing a local materials base reduces logistical risk and supports faster product introductions for device manufacturers across APAC.
- Africa’s threat landscape is evolving with rising connectivity and events such as Gitex Nigeria 2025 creating concentrated windows of exposure. Event organizers and ecosystem participants must treat such gatherings as critical infrastructure and apply enterprise grade security controls.
- Combining insights from both regions demonstrates a common pattern: scaled digital adoption must be accompanied by both defensive capacity and industrial capability building to ensure resilience.
- Operational priorities for practitioners:
  - For security teams in Africa focus on identity protections, endpoint hygiene, supply chain vetting for vendors, and rapid detection capabilities.
  - For semiconductor and materials stakeholders in India prioritize qualification pathways for localized materials, supply agreement frameworks, and joint innovation projects with device makers.
- Policy and investment levers matter. Government incentives and public private partnerships will shape whether market trajectories translate into sustainable manufacturing capacity and improved security posture.
