# Cyberattack on European Supermarkets Hacks Fridges, Raises Temperatures Dangerously High

## At a glance

European supermarkets have fallen victim to a cyberattack that has resulted in hacked refrigeration systems, raising temperatures to dangerous levels and spoiling food [[1](#ref-1)][[2](#ref-2)]. The extent of the damage is still being assessed, with some estimating losses in the millions [[4](#ref-4)]. The perpetrators, currently unidentified, have not publicly claimed responsibility [[5](#ref-5)].

## What happened

The cyberattack, which has affected hundreds of supermarkets across Europe, is believed to have originated from hacked refrigeration controllers, building management systems, door access panels, and point-of-sale gear [[1](#ref-1)]. Hackers have reportedly forced refrigeration units to undergo continuous defrost cycles and set temperatures as high as 122°F [[5](#ref-5)]. This has led to spoiled food and, in some instances, triggered fires [[5](#ref-5)][[6](#ref-6)].

The first reported incident occurred in France, where approximately 700 supermarkets were affected [[2](#ref-2)]. The Paris prosecutor's office is investigating the attack, which is estimated to have caused significant damage to the food industry [[2](#ref-2)]. Dutch supermarket giant Ahold Delhaize also reported a security issue and shut down its websites serving customers in Europe and the US [[2](#ref-2)].

These cyberattacks follow earlier disruptions in France, where hackers took control of cooling units at hundreds of supermarkets, leading to food losses and fires [[1](#ref-1)]. No one has claimed responsibility for these events, and it remains unclear if they are connected to the latest attacks.

## Why it matters

The cyberattack on European supermarkets underscores the growing threat of cybercrimes against critical infrastructure and the potential consequences for food security [[3](#ref-3)]. Experts warn that these attacks are becoming more sophisticated and could lead to significant economic losses.

## What remains unknown

The identity of the attackers and their motives remain unknown. It's also unclear if the attacks are connected to previous incidents in France. Additionally, the full extent of the damage caused by the cyberattack is still being assessed.
