## Executive Snapshot

**Rewritten Analysis: John Hancock Infrastructure Fund Q2 2025 Commentary**

This analysis synthesizes John Hancock Infrastructure Fund HPI Q2 2025 results, detailing who is involved, what happened, when and where it occurred, why the fund is positioned as a macro hedge, and how investors might interpret the data. The quarter shows modest earnings improvement and a resilient asset base that support a defensive investment posture anchored in infrastructure themes such as decarbonization and digitalization.

Key points

- The fund reported net investment income of **$0.265 per common share** for the quarter ended July 31, 2025, up from **$0.250** in the comparable quarter year over year.
- **Total managed assets** reached **$685.9 million** and **net assets** were **$428.8 million**.
- **NAV** stood at **$16.02 per share**.
- The data point to stability and reliable cash generation rather than rapid appreciation, supporting the thesis that infrastructure can serve as a macro hedge amid inflation and rate uncertainty.

## Q2 2025 Performance: The Numbers

**Quarterly results**

- Net investment income rose to **$0.265 per share**, signaling consistent cash generation from infrastructure exposures.
- Asset scale and discipline are evident in total managed assets of **$685.9 million** and net assets of **$428.8 million**.
- NAV steady at **$16.02 per share** indicates resilience in a volatile rate environment.

Interpretation

- The quarter reflects a defensive earnings profile focused on stable cash flows from essential infrastructure assets including utilities, energy systems, transportation and digital networks.
- Incremental improvement in income per share suggests portfolio cash flows remain resilient under inflationary pressure.

## Strategic Rationale: Infrastructure as a Macro Hedge

Core thesis

- Infrastructure assets deliver predictable regulated or contract backed revenues that cushion portfolios during periods of inflation and rate uncertainty.
- Long term structural drivers such as decarbonization, urbanization and digitalization reinforce demand for modern networks and energy systems.
- Public policy, notably the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act IIJA, mobilizes funding for clean energy, broadband expansion and related projects that create investment opportunities for funds like HPI.

Why this matters

- Essential services assets are less cyclical and can translate inflation linked cost pass through into revenues in some regulated frameworks.
- Infrastructure also provides diversification benefits due to lower correlation with traditional equities and fixed income.

## Why Infrastructure Outperforms in Uncertain Times

- Stable cash flows: Essential services generate predictable income reducing sensitivity to short term rate moves.
- Regulatory tailwinds: Government emphasis on modernization supports project economics and potential returns.
- Diversification: Lower correlation with other asset classes improves portfolio resilience during macro shocks.

## Risks and Considerations

- Project lead times and regulatory processes can delay value realization and affect timing of cash flow improvements.
- Rising rates increase financing costs and can pressure leveraged assets. HPI appears to manage leverage conservatively but investors should monitor leverage metrics closely.
- Limited portfolio disclosure in the Q2 notice necessitates scenario analysis and due diligence to assess concentration, embedded inflation exposure and interest rate sensitivity.

## Investment Thesis and Positioning

- HPI combines defensive cash generation with exposure to long term infrastructure growth themes.
- The fund offers income and preservation characteristics while retaining upside linked to decarbonization and digital infrastructure investment.
- Practical allocation guidance: a modest allocation such as five to ten percent to infrastructure oriented funds can lower overall portfolio volatility and improve resilience to macro shocks.

## Practical Guidance for Investors

- Income oriented investors can interpret the quarterly income of **$0.265** as a base yield component supporting current income in a high inflation environment.
- Growth oriented investors may use HPI as a defensive ballast that complements higher volatility holdings while maintaining exposure to secular trends in energy and broadband.
- Monitor NAV trends, income per share and any shifts in portfolio mix or leverage profile to update positioning.
